Resisting big tech

Let’s talk about what web 3.0.

The optimism of the world wide web that Sir Tim Berners Lee spun onto the world was a moment of joy. Watching it grow. Spinning my own lines. Connecting to others. It seemed, in the early 90s, to be a place for curiosity and shared learning. The first web page I made was for Stephen Hawking in 1994. People connected, ideas grew, new practices were born. Then came social media to fill the web with junk. People became increasingly divided. Advertising made everyone search for gold. Influences spun the gold of the web into their own threads. Sticky threads. Made to lure you in. To trap. Shared learning became shared unlearning. I said. He said. She said. I said. The greedy just wanted more and more. The world wide web and everything it stood for turned into a sticky greedy sickly pile of shit.

But it doesn’t have to be like that. Underneath all of this shit the idea of the web is still there. We just need to reclaim it. To bring back that optimism. To bring back shared learning. To bring back a sense of community.

Is this David Vs Goliath? Not at all. When I’m cooking I’m not cooking in anger. I’m cooking because I love food. I love making things that I and others can eat. I’m not trying to take down the greedy sticky fat filled sugar filled fast food giants. I’m just making food that I like. That nourishes me. That I try to learn what foods work. To learn ways of cooking not taught to me. Not passed down from my parents or grandparents (all actually pretty bad cooks… sorry mum, dad, grandpa, nanna, granny… you did your best). I’m not going to romanticise their cooking. It wasn’t great. I learnt that early and started to cook for myself as soon as I could. As I was learning to cook, I was also learning to code and express myself.

Here’s my origin story.

I have tasted power. Real power. The kind of power that I guess drives Big Tech Bros. Jon Rogers aged 11 was given a ZX Spectrum computer. I was driving my parents (and large family - I’m the middle of five) nuts. Asthmatic. Filled with steroids. Scratchy. Wheezy. Weedy. Allergic to everything. My teacher (the very wonderful Mrs Mason of Benson Primary School) described me as a bottle of fizzy pop (that’s juice in scotland and soda in the US) that was shaken up and nobody could get the lid off. I’m still that person. If you know me, you’ll know. But that ZX spectrum, it focused me in ways nothing (except cooking) could. It was a computer you plugged into the TV and you wrote code and graphics and sounds leap out of the screen. Your graphics. Your sounds. But on the family TV. The ONLY family TV. In 1982 there were two content providers for our TV. The BBC and ITV. But on that day in 1982 everything changed for the Rogers household. The TV had three content providers. The BBC, ITV and me. “Mum Jon’s doing weird shit with the TV again”. “Dad I want to watch, but Jon’s typing at it”. “Mum Jon’s got the TV saying “Fuck off!”... and so on. the POWER. O to the M to the G. It felt good people. Weedy, scratchy, wheezy Jon - the power in my hands. “Ah but mum and dad I’m learning….”. And I did. What a thing of joy. A thing of power. Is this what a pen felt like in the hands of Shakespeare?

Then came electronics. You could make your own things that connected to the TV. By then it was a dedicated display. A midi controller. My casio electronic piano could be played by a file on my computer. Big Track hacked. Later, at uni, a robot arm to pick up concrete slabs (all those summers at Rogers Concrete). Making making making. Connecting. Building. Learning.

Then came the web. The coffee pot at Cambridge Computer Science Lab that we could watch daily. Neural networks. Making things start to learn. A switch to teaching electronics at the Royal College of Art. Rethinking everything you could ever think about a computer. Gillian Crampton Smith , Rory Hamilton, Bill Gaver, Fiona Raby, Tony Dunne. Thinking in ways I’d never seen in engineering school. Thinking through making. Making through thinking. The optimism. The web. Things. Infinite possibilities. My career as a creative tech lit up. Given life.

It wasn’t until many years later walking through one of the connecting tunnels on London's underground with Michcelle Thorne, that something dawned on me. It was that pivot. I’d joined her as a senior Fellow with Mozilla in 2016. The web and everything that the internet stood for had gone to shit. Surveillance capitalism was thriving. Manipulating us. Getting richer. Getting more powerful. All of those years teaching the opportunities afforded by the internet. Was I wrong? The answer - Jein (Mein Lieblingswort auf Deutsch!). Ja, opportunities are important. Why be a lecturer or professor if this isn’t about generating opportunities for our students through learning. But, and here’s the “nein”, it was clear that it was reckless to teach and research opportunities without countering that with responsibilities. Opportunities and responsibilities. A cycle. A learning cycle.

Then came large language model AI. The hungry caterpillar of all computing. A very hungry caterpillar. Eating and eating. On day One it ate one apple. On day Two it ate the web. All of it. And what Eric Carle didn’t tell the millions of parents that read that book to children, was just how much shit that caterpillar must have given out. The very hungry LLM it’s shit is everywhere. It’s suffocating the web. Killing it. Search - gone. Shared learning - gone. Optimism - gone. Covered in dis- and misinformed- excrement. There is no community. Video did kill the radio star.

But stop right there Mr Me.

All is not lost! It’s now ours to reclaim. Let’s just start to create webpages. To buy up domain names (as David Chatting yesterday declared) like it’s 2005! To express ourselves again. To connect. Form community. Reclaim the web - not as David fighting Goliath (real world - Goliath didn’t even notice), but as a third space. Our space.

Web 3.0. It’s a third space between Big Tech and despair. It’s where hope lives. Just a few connections at a time. Thank you Sir Tim Berners Lee - we are back!
